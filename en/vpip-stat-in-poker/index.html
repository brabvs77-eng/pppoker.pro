The most important stat in poker: VPIP

Author: Roman Shaposhnikov

Date: 12.01.2026

Online players who use a HUD (Heads-Up Display) have many stats to choose from, but the most important is VPIP. This preflop stat reflects how often a player voluntarily puts money into the pot whenever they have the chance.

Understanding and using VPIP is the first step toward profitable poker.

What is VPIP?

VPIP (short for "Voluntarily Put money In Pot") measures how loose (wide range) or tight (narrow range) a player is.

VPIP includes limps, calls, raises, and 3-bets — any preflop action except a fold.

Formula and example

The VPIP formula is:

VPIP = Times the player voluntarily put money in the pot / (Total hands – Folds on BB and SB)

 

Hands where everyone folds to the Big Blind (BB) and BB wins without a voluntary action are excluded.

Example: If a player voluntarily put money in 20 of 100 hands, their VPIP is 20%.

VPIP on the blinds: the key word is "voluntary"

It is important to understand that a blind (BB or SB) is a forced bet. It does not count toward VPIP unless the player takes an additional voluntary action.

  • Small Blind (SB): If the SB folds without putting extra money in voluntarily, VPIP = 0% for that hand.
  • Big Blind (BB): If everyone limps to BB and BB checks without adding money, VPIP = 0%.
  • BB calls or raises: If BB calls a raise or raises over limps, they voluntarily add money — VPIP = 100% for that hand.
  • BB checks then bets postflop: If BB checks preflop but bets on the flop (or check-calls/check-raises), they voluntarily put money in — VPIP = 100% for that hand.

Player type by VPIP

VPIP is the basis for classifying opponents. The more hands (samples) you have (3,000+ is best), the more accurate your reads.

VPIP range (6-max) Player type Description
> 40% Fish / Maniac Very high. Enters pots too often, usually with weak hands.
28% – 40% Loose / LAG Wide range that needs an aggressive response (3-bets, 4-bets).
18% – 26% TAG (Tight Aggressive) Optimal for most games. Strong hands played aggressively.
< 16% Nit / Extremely Tight Very low. Enters pots only with strong cards.

6-max vs full ring

Players at 6-max tables usually have higher VPIP than at full-ring tables. A solid TAG might show ~15% VPIP full ring and ~20% at 6-max.

Important: With fewer than 2,000 hands, VPIP can be misleading — do not jump to conclusions.

Conclusion: how to use VPIP

VPIP is the most useful stat and updates every hand. Quick data on how loose or tight someone plays helps you:

  • Estimate their range strength more accurately.
  • Choose better lines to exploit them.

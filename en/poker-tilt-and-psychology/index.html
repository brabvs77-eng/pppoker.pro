Tilt is when emotions start making decisions for you. A bad beat on the river, a lost coin flip on the bubble, an "obvious" fold that turned out to be a bluff — and suddenly you are on three tables with -EV 3-bets. Sound familiar? Here is how to spot tilt before your whole bankroll is gone.

What is tilt

Tilt is a state where frustration, anger, or euphoria distort your judgment. You play suboptimally: bluff where you should fold, call where you should raise, move up stakes to "get even".

Tilt is not a character flaw. It is a normal brain response to uncertainty and unfairness. Pros tilt too — the difference is they notice it sooner.

Signs: you are on tilt if…

  • You play more tables than planned
  • You move up after a loss
  • You open CRASH or other side games to "win it back"
  • You type in chat things you regret later
  • You cannot remember why you made a specific river call
  • You feel "today is not my day" — and keep playing

7 ways to stop bleeding your stack

1. Session stop-loss

Before you start, decide how many buy-ins you can lose. 3–4 — stop. Close the client. No "one more hand". Bankroll rules — bankroll management guide.

2. Pause after a big bad beat

Lost 3+ buy-ins in one hand? Stand up, get water, 5 minutes without poker. Your brain needs time to exit "revenge" mode.

3. Do not play tired

After 4+ hours, decision quality drops 20–30%. Better to end in profit and sleep than give it back on a night session.

4. Separate result from decision

A +EV call that lost is a good decision. A -EV bluff that won is bad. Judge the process, not the outcome. See EV.

5. One table on tilt

Feel irritated — close all tables except one. Multi-tabling on tilt multiplies mistakes.

6. No "win it back" in other formats

Lost in an MTT — do not rush to CRASH or Russian poker to recover. Each format has its own bankroll. CRASH limits — separate guide.

7. Review hands with a clear head

After the session (not during!) review 2–3 controversial hands. Not "how could he play like that" but "what could I do differently". This trains rational thinking.

Tilt in tournaments vs cash

Cash: tilt shows as aggression and overplaying hands. Fix — stop-loss and move down.

MTT: tilt often after busting on the bubble or in ITM with a short stack. Fix — do not register for an immediate rematch; pause 15–30 minutes. Picking tournaments — NUTS tournament schedule.

Player types and tilt

TAG on tilt becomes LAG. Fish on tilt — super-aggressor. Nit on tilt — leaves the table and registers for a turbo with half the bankroll. Know your pattern — that is half the win. Overview — Main player types.

When to take a longer break

If poker stopped being fun, you play with money you cannot afford to lose, or tilt lasts weeks — take a break. A week without poker beats a month rebuilding your bankroll.

Conclusion

Tilt is inevitable. Bleeding your bankroll because of tilt is not. Stop-losses, pauses, separating formats, and judging decisions not results — seven simple tools that work better than any lucky charm.

Starting Hands in Texas Hold'em: What to Play

Author: Roman Shaposhnikov

Date: 12.01.2026

Every Hold'em hand starts the same — you get two cards. Which cards you choose to play shapes everything that follows. Preflop decisions often decide your stack long before the river.

Let's look at which starting hands are strong, which are marginal, and which you should fold immediately.

What are starting-hand charts and why use them?

A starting-hand chart shows which hands to enter the pot with from different positions. It does not guarantee profit in every hand, but gives a statistical edge over time.

Beginners often make two mistakes:

  1. Playing too many hands (loose): They end up in tough spots with trash and lose money.
  2. Waiting only for premiums (tight): They bleed blinds and become too readable.

A chart is not dogma but a guide. It shows which range is mathematically profitable to raise, call, or fold depending on position.

Best starting hands (premium range)

Premium hands win the largest share of pots preflop. With them you should usually raise.

  • Absolute monsters: AA, KK, QQ, JJ
  • Top suited hands: AKs (ace-king suited)
  • Strong broadways: AQs, TT, AKo

Extended range: hands for active play

After premiums come strong but non-monster hands suitable to raise in most spots, especially from late position:

  • Medium pairs: 99, 88, 77
  • Strong aces and kings: AQo, AJs, ATs, KQs, KJs
  • Suited connectors: T9s, 98s, 87s

These often make strong combinations: top pair with a good kicker, flush draws, or straight draws.

Acceptable hands: play with caution

These can be profitable only in specific conditions (late position, cheap multiway entry, or weak opponents):

  • Pocket pairs below 77 (66, 55…)
  • Weak suited aces like A5s, A4s
  • Wide connectors like J9s, T8s

If you miss the flop, fold to avoid costly mistakes on later streets.

Hands you should almost always fold

Over time these hands drain your stack even if you win one big pot. Fold them in 99% of cases:

  • Offsuit trash with no connectivity: T4o, J6o, 92o.
  • Weak kickers: K2o, Q5o, A8o.
  • Most «pretty but useless» hands like J9o or K8o.

What affects your opening range?

Your opening range is not fixed — it depends on several key factors:

FactorHow it affects play
PositionThe closer you are to the Button (Dealer), the wider you can open — you have positional edge postflop (except SB and BB).
Stack sizeSmaller stacks simplify your range (often push/fold).
Opponent typeAgainst Nits you can steal wider; against Maniacs sit and wait for strong hands.
Action before youIf there was already a raise, hands up to «active play» range usually fold.

Summary

  1. Simply not playing trash already makes you stronger than most recreational players.
  2. Even a weak player who selects hands well wins more than someone who «likes to play everything».

Stay disciplined preflop — it gives you a big edge over time.

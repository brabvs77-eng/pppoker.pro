Overlay — when the guarantee exceeds total buy-ins. That is your value: the club tops up from its own fund. The bigger the overlay relative to buy-in, the better the tournament mathematically.

Tournament types in the NUTS schedule

  • Freezeout — one entry, no re-entry. Discipline and ICM from hand one
  • Re-entry / Rebuy — you can buy back in. Field is softer, prize pool grows
  • PKO (Progressive Knockout) — bounty for each knockout. Aggressive style pays off
  • Turbo / Hyper — fast blinds. Short stack, little time for decisions
  • Deepstack — deep starting stack. More postflop play

MTT basics — Multi-Table Tournaments (MTT). For micro stakes — microstakes strategy.

How to find value: 5 practical tips

  1. Watch the overlay. A $500 GTD with 30 players at $5 beats $200 GTD with 50 players at $10
  2. Use late registration wisely. Fewer levels — less time, but a shorter stack. Works for turbos, not deepstacks
  3. Re-entry is not always +EV. Busted on the bubble with a short stack — sometimes saving the money is better
  4. Pick your time. Moscow evening and weekends — more recreational players, softer field
  5. Follow promos. Mini highrollers and series with boosted GTD — promo example

Bankroll and buy-in choice

Do not play tournaments at the edge of your bankroll. Rule: 100+ ABI for your schedule. Details — bankroll management. Rakeback and bonuses add ROI: NUTS rakeback.

Common mistakes when reading the schedule

  • Registering for everything without considering the final table time
  • Ignoring blind structure (turbo with a deepstack bankroll — bad idea)
  • Playing 5 MTTs at once on a bankroll that covers one
  • Missing Telegram announcements — best guarantees appear there first

Conclusion

The NUTS tournament schedule is not chaos — it is a calendar of opportunities. Read GTD, structure, late reg, and overlay — then pick tournaments where the math is on your side. And if a final clashes with dinner — blame the calendar. We are working on it.

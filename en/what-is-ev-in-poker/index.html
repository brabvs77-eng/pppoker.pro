EV (Expected Value) is the average result of a decision if the same spot repeated many times. +EV lines print in the long run; −EV lines bleed. Folding has EV = 0 (you neither win nor lose more).

Formula and a basic calculation

Simple two-outcome form:

EV = (win probability × amount won) − (lose probability × amount lost)

Win and lose probabilities add to 1 when those are the only outcomes.

Example. Pot $100, opponent shoves $50, you have a flush draw with about 20% equity.

Calling $50: you win $150 when you hit, lose $50 when you miss.

EV = 0.2 × $150 − 0.8 × $50 = $30 − $40 = −$10

The call is −EV; on average you lose $10 in this spot. Folding is better unless implied odds change the math.

Using EV at the table

Follow the math, not the mood. If a call is clearly +EV — call. If a shove is −EV — fold.

EV ≠ short-term profit. You can run bad on +EV lines for a long time.

If call EV ≤ 0, folding is usually correct (fold EV = 0).

You will not solve every spot live. Study with equity tools offline; in-game lean on outs, pot odds, and simple heuristics.

Tournaments vs cash

Cash: chip EV ≈ money EV. Take +EV lines.

MTT / SnG: ICM and future stack value matter. A slightly +chip-EV all-in can be −$EV. Early, avoid thin gambles even if pure chip math looks fine.

Common EV mistakes

• Treating EV as abstract and ignoring opponent tendencies
• Forgetting implied odds and later-street payoffs
• Playing a +EV strategy with a bankroll that cannot survive variance

How to train EV

Review hands offline with real equities. Use simulators and equity calculators. Then stress-test ideas at private PPPoker tables — for example in the NUTS club.

Summary

EV is a tool, not a superstition. Players who calculate and apply it consistently win without needing “luck” as a strategy.

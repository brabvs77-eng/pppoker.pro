EV (Expected Value) is the mathematical expectation of the result of an action in poker. Simply put, EV shows how much you win or lose on average when the same situation repeats many times. Over the long run, +EV decisions bring profit, −EV bring loss. Fold EV is always zero.

Formula and basic calculation

Standard formula:

EV = Chance to win × Winnings if you win − Chance to lose × Bet if you lose

Win/lose probabilities sum to 1.

Example. Pot $100, opponent shoves $50, you have a flush draw with 20% (0.2) equity.

If you call $50, you win $150 when you win and lose $50 when you lose.

Plug in: EV = 0.2 × $150 − 0.8 × $50 = $30 − $40 = −$10

Conclusion: the call is −EV; on average you lose $10 in similar spots.

Practical EV use at the table

  1. Rely on math, not emotion. If a call is +EV — call; if a shove is −EV — fold.
  2. EV ≠ instant profit. Even with +EV you can be down over a short sample.
  3. EV of a fold = 0. So if expected call EV ≤ 0, folding is often correct.
  4. Do not calculate every spot by hand. Use HUD/software off-table; in real time use simple heuristics and frequency (outs / pot odds).

EV in tournaments vs cash

Cash — direct EV comparison: +EV = play.

MTT / SnG — extra factors appear (ICM, future stack value). A slightly positive chip EV line can be negative in ICM terms. Early in a tournament it is often better to avoid marginal all-ins even if they are slightly +EV in pure math.

Common EV mistakes

  • Playing abstract EV without opponent reads and table dynamics.
  • Ignoring implied odds and hidden future payoff on later streets (cash AoF or deep stacks).
  • Neglecting bankroll management: even a +EV strategy needs enough roll to survive variance.

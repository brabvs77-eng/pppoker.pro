are a great way for beginners and experienced players to enjoy poker and compete for real prizes without risking a large share of their bankroll.

Most players start with microstakes tournaments because they help you learn the rules, get comfortable on an online platform, and test new tactics.

Dynamics of large fields

Microstakes tournaments often draw hundreds or even thousands of players, which can significantly boost the prize pool. However, large fields lower your chances of reaching the money (ITM), making the final table, and winning. That is why it is important to understand how to play as efficiently as possible. Key qualities for success: patience, discipline, and strategic awareness.

⏳ Early stage of an MTT

In the early stages of a tournament it is crucial to understand preflop open-raise ranges. Playing a tight range helps you avoid unnecessary chip losses. For example, folding A♥️J♠️ from early position at a 9-max table is acceptable with deep stacks.

ABC poker strategy includes: tight-aggressive style (TAG), using positional advantage, and value betting.

  • TAG: make continuation bets in heads-up pots when you are one-on-one with an opponent after the preflop.
  • Table position:
    • Early position: enter the pot only with strong hands.
    • Middle position: you can widen your range slightly, but still play strong hands.
    • Late position: try to steal blinds as often as possible.

Recommended open-raise size: 2–3.5 BB.

Middle stage of an MTT

At this stage blinds have grown significantly and average stacks have shrunk. Widening your range lets you play more pots. For example, add suited connectors like 98s, 87s, low pairs, and weak Axs hands from any position.

Watch table dynamics and opponent styles. If someone plays too loose, use your aggressive hands for value betting. Stay disciplined — winning the tournament is still a long way off.

Recommended open-raise size: 2–2.5 BB.

Playing the bubble

The bubble is the stage right before the money. The next player out finishes last without a payout.

  • Short stack: play more carefully to survive and reach the money.
  • Medium stack: avoid big pots against large stacks. Pressure short stacks that want to preserve their chips.
  • Large stack: use your advantage to pressure medium and short stacks and build your stack.

Late stage of an MTT

Keep building your stack to move closer to the final table.

  • Stack building: pressure medium and short stacks while avoiding big pots against other chip leaders.
  • Blind control: track how many BB (big blinds) you have. With more than 30 BB you can afford more strategic moves. Below 20 BB, switch to push-fold (all-in or fold).
  • Do not overvalue hands: be ready to fold even top pair or medium-strength hands when you sense danger.

Final table strategy

The final table is the most important stage of a tournament.

  • ICM: account for the Independent Chip Model, which estimates the monetary value of each chip based on the remaining prize pool and stack distribution.
  • Large stack: pressure medium and short stacks and play aggressively — they have a harder time taking risks.
  • Medium stack: pick your spots carefully to double up and look for blind and ante steals.
  • Short stack: play aggressively to double up and use fold equity (the chance opponents fold to your all-in).

Key points: watch opponents, find their weaknesses, factor in ICM, and value bet.

✅ Key tips

  • Patience and discipline: do not take unnecessary risks; wait for strong hands.
  • Tight-aggressive play in early stages: play a narrow range aggressively.
  • Middle stage: widen your range and exploit opponent mistakes.
  • Bubble: adjust your play based on stack size.
  • Late stage: accumulate chips and watch your stack-to-blind ratio.
  • Final table: factor in ICM and adjust ranges.

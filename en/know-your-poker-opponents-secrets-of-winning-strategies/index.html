Online poker is a mix of strategy and psychology. Reading player types is one of the fastest ways to raise your win rate: each style has predictable habits and leaks.

This guide covers five common archetypes — calling station, maniac, nit, TAG (tight-aggressive), and LAG (loose-aggressive) — and how to play against each of them.

Use these reads in cash games and tournaments alike. At the NUTS club on PPPoker, you will meet every type below — often in the same session.

1. Calling station

Traits

A calling station is passive but active in volume: they see a lot of flops and prefer calling to raising. Hand-reading is often weak, so they continue with many second-best hands.

How to play them

Value bet thinner and more often. Bluffs land less; they call light. Keep ranges weighted toward strong made hands, size up for value, and skip fancy multi-street bluffs that need folds.

2. Maniac

Traits

A maniac plays a huge preflop range and keeps betting, raising, and 4-betting. Results swing hard — high variance is the point.

How to play them

Stay calm and wait for premium spots. Trap more, call down lighter with strong but not nut hands, and do not tilt into their chaos. Let them bluff into you when you have it.

3. Nit

Traits

A nit plays very few hands and usually only continues with strong cards. Stacks are often deep because they avoid marginal spots.

How to play them

Steal blinds and small pots aggressively. Fold equity is high when you pressure them. If they suddenly show interest — three-bet, large barrel, check-raise — give them credit and tighten up.

4. Tight-aggressive (TAG)

Traits

TAGs open a selective range and play those hands hard. They raise more than they call and usually keep a healthy stack.

How to play them

Respect strength, especially out of position. Use position, track their open and 3-bet sizes, and look for small exploitable patterns rather than hero-calling their obvious value lines.

5. Loose-aggressive (LAG)

Traits

LAGs play more hands than TAGs but still apply pressure — frequent 3-bets and barrels, without the pure chaos of a maniac.

How to play them

Fight back selectively: call more in position, 4-bet value and some blockers, and punish over-bluffing on later streets. Patience plus timely aggression takes their equity.

Conclusion

Labeling opponents is not labeling forever — samples matter — but a working taxonomy helps you choose lines faster. Adapt when the data contradicts the first read.

What is GTO?

GTO (Game Theory Optimal) is an approach to decision-making in poker aimed at making your style «unexploitable» by opponents. Simply put: GTO is a way to balance ranges and actions (bet/check/call/raise/fold) so an opponent cannot systematically profit by adjusting to your habits.

GTO treats poker as an optimization problem: for each game situation there are mathematically grounded strategies that maximize expected value over the long run when the opponent makes no obvious mistakes.

Brief history and context

Game theory ideas have applied to poker for a long time — from mid-20th-century theory to practical solvers in the 21st century. Online poker and software (PioSolver, etc.) made it possible to compute approximate equilibria for many typical spots. GTO concepts then spread widely in pro training.

How to study GTO — a practical path

  1. Preflop charts — start here: RFI, 3-bet, blind defense. This is the foundation; postflop GTO is weak without it.
  2. Study ranges — think in opponent ranges and your own range, not a single hand.
  3. Simple solver spots — review typical flops and c-bet strategies. Do not jump into complex multiway pots immediately.
  4. Practice and review — save hands, compare your lines to solver output, and adjust ranges.
  5. Combine GTO and exploit gradually — learn when to deviate from baseline strategy for extra profit.

Blockers: a simple, powerful idea

A blocker is a card in your hand that reduces the chance your opponent holds a specific strong combination. Example: you hold A♣K♥ on J♣9♣8♦ — the A♣ blocks part of villain’s strong flush ranges. Using blockers helps you:

  • bluff more confidently when you «block» potential responses;
  • pick bet/raise sizes more accurately;
  • build bluff and value ranges that stay balanced against opposing combinations.

Blockers are a key tool in modern postflop thinking and an important part of the GTO toolkit.

GTO vs exploit play — how to combine them

GTO gives you a baseline «safe» strategy that is hard to exploit systematically.

Exploit means adjusting to specific opponent weaknesses (e.g. if they fold too often after your 3-bet, you widen bluff ranges).

Strong players keep a solid GTO base and deviate when opponents clearly make mistakes. Going too exploit-heavy without understanding balanced baseline play makes you vulnerable against other advanced players.

Should you study GTO?

Yes. GTO is not an end in itself or a magic formula, but without it you lose control over how optimal your decisions are. Studying GTO:

  • improves how you see ranges;
  • shows when and how far you can lean toward exploit;
  • raises overall win rate over time when applied well.

Start with fundamentals — preflop charts and simple postflop spots — and move on as you are ready.

Practice in the NUTS club

The NUTS club on PPPoker is a convenient place to test GTO ideas: fast cash, multi-format tournaments, SPIN and MTT formats, and specialized tables (e.g. Bomb Pot) where you can test range balance in unusual conditions. Practical tips:

  • train at low and mid limits, then move up;
  • save hands and review them in a solver or with a coach;
  • use NUTS side games for specific spots (multiway pots, short-deck, etc.).

Conclusion

GTO is not dogma but a tool: a strong mathematical base for building unexploitable strategy and confidently mixing in your own exploits. Studying GTO improves decision quality and gives flexibility against different opponent types. Practice, review, and apply — the NUTS club is a good arena for that.

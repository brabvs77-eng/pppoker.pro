In poker every decision should have a mathematical basis. One of the most important tools with drawing hands is implied odds — potential pot odds showing how much you can win in the future if your hand completes.

Pot odds and implied odds: the difference

Pot odds are direct pot odds: the ratio of current pot size to the bet you must call.

Implied odds are a forecast of possible winnings on later streets if you make a strong hand. Unlike pot odds, they cannot be calculated exactly: they depend on opponent style, board texture, and your position.

Examples from practice

Good implied odds
Your hand — 7♦6♠
Flop — K♥5♠8♣

You have an open-ended straight draw. Even if pot odds look unfavorable, when you hit the straight opponents will struggle to read your hand and you get paid on the turn or river.

Bad implied odds
Your hand — K♣4♠
Flop — Q♦J♥T♠

Here the draw is obvious. If an A or 9 comes, the board screams straights. Opponents fold easily and you win nothing extra.

How to apply implied odds

  • With good implied odds you can call even with weak pot odds.
  • If they are weak, rely only on direct odds.
  • In all-in spots implied odds disappear because there are no further bets.

Calculation example

You have a flush draw. Opponent bets $10 into a $10 pot.

  • Pot = $20. Call $10. Pot odds = 2:1.
  • Chance to hit the flush on one street ≈ 4.2:1.
  • Required implied odds = 4.2 − 2 = 2.2:1.

You need to win at least $22 more for the call to be profitable.

Pot odds vs implied odds

Pot odds (direct) — ratio of pot size to the amount you must call right now.

Implied odds (potential) — estimate of extra winnings on later streets if you make a strong hand.

Main difference: pot odds are exact; implied odds are a forecast based on opponent style, board, and position.

Good implied odds spot

Example:

Your hand — 7♦6♠

Flop — K♥5♠8♣

You have an open-ended straight draw. If an opponent bets, you can call even with bad pot odds because when you hit, your hand is hard to read and you win more chips on later streets.

Bad implied odds spot

Example:

Your hand — K♣4♠

Flop — Q♦J♥T♠

You again have an open-ended straight draw, but the situation differs. If an A or 9 comes, the board becomes too obvious and scary. Opponents fold more easily and you get no extra payoff — weak or zero implied odds.

Using implied odds in play

With good implied odds you can call bets with bad pot odds.

With weak implied odds play only by direct pot odds.

In all-in there are no implied odds because no future streets exist.

Implied odds calculation (example)

You have a flush draw. Opponent bets $10 into a $10 pot.

Pot = $20. Call $10. Pot odds = 2:1.

Chance to hit flush on one street ≈ 4.2:1.

Required implied odds = 4.2 − 2 = 2.2:1.

You need to win at least $22 more on later streets for the call to be profitable.

Conclusion

Implied odds help when direct pot odds do not justify a call. If you read opponents and ranges well, you can use potential odds and turn marginal spots into profit.
